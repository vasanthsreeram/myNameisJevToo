

To determine whether passenger N. Abernathy will miss the connection, we must calculate the probability of a successful connection based on the provided evidence and rules.

**1. Analyze the Connection Rules**
Let $L_{in}$ be the delay of the inbound flight (SK 1462) and $L_{out}$ be the delay of the outbound flight (SK 2019).
The passenger makes the connection if:
*   **Case 1:** $L_{out} < 20$ minutes (Outbound is "on time") AND $L_{in} \le 40$ minutes.
*   **Case 2:** $L_{out} \ge 20$ minutes (Outbound is "late") AND $L_{in} \le 60$ minutes.

The passenger **misses** the connection if:
*   **Case 3:** $L_{out} < 20$ minutes AND $L_{in} > 40$ minutes.
*   **Case 4:** $L_{out} \ge 20$ minutes AND $L_{in} > 60$ minutes.

**2. Determine Probabilities from Evidence**

*   **Outbound Flight (SK 2019):**
    *   $P(L_{out} \ge 20) = 0.25$ (25% of days)
    *   $P(L_{out} < 20) = 1 - 0.25 = 0.75$ (75% of days, "on time")

*   **Inbound Flight (SK 1462):**
    Based on the last 50 operations:
    *   $P(L_{in} \le 40) = 34/50 = 0.68$
    *   $P(40 < L_{in} \le 60) = 6/50 = 0.12$
    *   $P(L_{in} > 60) = 10/50 = 0.20$
    *   Therefore, $P(L_{in} > 40) = 0.12 + 0.20 = 0.32$
    *   And $P(L_{in} > 60) = 0.20$

**3. Calculate Probability of Missing the Connection**

Since the delays are independent, we can multiply the probabilities for each scenario.

*   **Probability of Missing (Case 3):** Outbound on time AND Inbound > 40 mins late.
    $$P(\text{Miss}_3) = P(L_{out} < 20) \times P(L_{in} > 40)$$
    $$P(\text{Miss}_3) = 0.75 \times 0.32 = 0.24$$

*   **Probability of Missing (Case 4):** Outbound late AND Inbound > 60 mins late.
    $$P(\text{Miss}_4) = P(L_{out} \ge 20) \times P(L_{in} > 60)$$
    $$P(\text{Miss}_4) = 0.25 \times 0.20 = 0.05$$

*   **Total Probability of Missing:**
    $$P(\text{Miss}) = P(\text{Miss}_3) + P(\text{Miss}_4)$$
    $$P(\text{Miss}) = 0.24 + 0.05 = 0.29$$

**4. Calculate Probability of Making the Connection**

$$P(\text{Make}) = 1 - P(\text{Miss})$$
$$P(\text{Make}) = 1 - 0.29 = 0.71$$

**5. Conclusion**

*   Probability of making the connection: **71%**
*   Probability of missing the connection: **29%**

The question asks: "Will passenger N. Abernathy miss the connection...?"
While there is a significant risk (29%), the most likely outcome based on the probabilities is that the passenger **makes** the connection (71% > 50%). In binary choice questions regarding future events based on probability, the answer corresponds to the most probable outcome.

The travel agent's note ("one-in-three chance of misconnecting") is an approximation of the 29% risk, but it does not mean the passenger *will* miss it. The statistical expectation is that the connection is made.

Therefore, the correct classification for the most likely outcome is that the passenger makes the connection.

Answer: **A. no - The passenger makes the connection.**
