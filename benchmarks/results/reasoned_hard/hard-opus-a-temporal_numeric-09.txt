

To determine if the dose was on schedule, we must calculate the **actual elapsed time** between the two doses, ignoring the time zone change on the patient's watch.

**1. Identify the times in a single time zone (Chicago/UTC-5):**

*   **Previous Dose:**
    *   Taken: 3 September, 13:00 Chicago time.
*   **Current Dose (On board):**
    *   Taken: 4 September, 01:30 **Madrid time** (UTC+2).
    *   Convert to Chicago time (UTC-5):
        *   Madrid is 7 hours ahead of Chicago (UTC+2 vs UTC-5).
        *   $01:30 \text{ (Madrid)} - 7 \text{ hours} = 18:30 \text{ (Chicago time on 3 September)}$.

**2. Calculate Elapsed Time:**

*   From 13:00 Chicago time to 18:30 Chicago time.
*   $18:30 - 13:00 = 5 \text{ hours and } 30 \text{ minutes}$.

**3. Compare with Dosing Rule:**

*   The rule states a dose is "on schedule" if taken **no earlier than 11 hours** and **no later than 13 hours** after the previous dose.
*   The actual elapsed time was **5.5 hours**.
*   5.5 hours is significantly less than the minimum 11-hour window.

**Conclusion:**

The patient mistakenly believed 12.5 hours had passed because they looked at their watch (which had been set to Madrid time) without accounting for the time difference. In reality, only 5.5 hours had passed. Taking a dose this soon after the previous one is "too close together" and outside the safe window.
